## 5. Mean Normalization

This is the next topic in Week 2. The main purpose here is to make **collaborative filtering work better, especially for a new user**. ([Coursera][1])

### The problem

Imagine our movie ratings are:

| Movie   | Alice | Bob | Charlie |
| ------- | ----: | --: | ------: |
| Movie A |     5 |   4 |       5 |
| Movie B |     4 |   3 |       4 |
| Movie C |     3 |   2 |       3 |

Now a **new user, Eve**, has rated nothing yet.

Without normalization, the model initially has no information about Eve and can end up predicting **0 for all movies**, which is not a useful starting prediction. ([DeepLearning.AI][2])

---

### Mean normalization fixes this

For each movie, calculate its average rating.

For Movie A:

$$
\mu_A = \frac{5+4+5}{3}=4.67
$$

Then subtract that mean from each rating:

$$
y_{\text{normalized}} = y-\mu
$$

So for Movie A:

$$
5-4.67=0.33
$$

$$
4-4.67=-0.67
$$

$$
5-4.67=0.33
$$

Now the ratings represent:

> **How much higher or lower than the movie's average rating did this user rate it?**

---

### Why is this useful?

After normalization, a new user with no ratings starts around the **average rating for each movie**, rather than starting at zero.

So conceptually:

```text
Without normalization:
New user → 0, 0, 0, 0 ...

With normalization:
New user → movie averages
             ↓
          much more reasonable starting point
```

The learned model predicts the **deviation from the mean**, and the mean is added back to get the final predicted rating. ([joan kusuma][3])

### 🧠 The key idea

> **Mean normalization subtracts each item's average rating from its observed ratings, making the model learn deviations from the average instead of starting from zero.**

And one important distinction from ordinary feature scaling:

**Here, mean normalization is being used specifically within the recommender-system formulation to center the rating data; it is not the same thing as standardizing every feature to unit variance.** ([hadiahameed.github.io][4])
